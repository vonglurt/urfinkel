# UR FINKEL 2026.09.2711 — no crash after a win, a piece home is an event

**The first stable release of UR FINKEL**, after a round of bug fixes and
play testing: both bugs players reported (#1, #2) are fixed below. This is
the version to download - the build on `main` can be ahead of it, and the
next stable version will be released here with its own number.

**UR FINKEL** is the Royal Game of Ur — the 4,500-year-old race game from the
royal tombs at Ur — as Irving Finkel reconstructed its rules, for the
Commodore Plus/4.

It plays inside a cabinet. Boot lands on a theatre front — frieze bands,
chasing lamps, a proscenium — and the curtains open on a royal fanfare to
reveal the menu. Four modes (vs URBOT, two players, two players with real
lots, URBOT demo), a rules screen, tumbling tetrahedral lots, the full
Finkel ruleset, URBOT narrating its doctrine turn by turn, and a victory
that pours a gold trophy and engraves the winner's name into its bowl. The
music is 19 transcribed duets written for TED's two voices.

**▶ [Play it in your browser](https://vonglurt.github.io/urfinkel/)** — no install.
Recordings of the big moments are on that page and in the
[README](https://github.com/vonglurt/urfinkel#in-motion).

## What's new in 2026.09.2711

**The crash after a win or a loss is fixed** (#2). Moving a piece up or
left made cc65 divide a negative number as unsigned, so the piece was drawn
45 rows off the screen — into the program's own code. The match carried on;
the damaged code first ran on the menu afterwards, and the machine jammed.
Every build before this one has it.

**Both voices from the first bar** (#1). Every written song now plays both
parts from power-on.

**A piece borne off is an event.** The laser writes *bear them off the board
to your home*, the count is cut in block letters — *1 home*, *6 home* — and
both sides' race is shown: pieces home out of seven, and pips still to go.

**Against URBOT, URBOT is player one** and sits on top; you play from the
near side of the board.

**Captures are quicker** — a quarter of the time they took — and **the
fireworks after a win run for five minutes**, now timed by the clock rather
than by the loop. Any key ends them. The half of every burst that flew left
or up was never drawn before; it is now.

**Versions are the build stamp.** The menu shows `yyyy.mm.ddHH`, and so does
the release.

## Tested

- **This exact build, soaked in VICE** through three URBOT matches in a
  row. Every victory went back to the menu and started the next match. A
  watchpoint on the whole program saw no write into its code, and nothing
  reached past 256 bytes of the (now 1.5 KB) C stack. Left alone, the
  fireworks lasted 15 171 frames - 303 seconds; a key pressed during them
  ended them at once.
- **The crash, before and after:** the unfixed build jams at `$38FA`, the
  address it was reported at; five soaks of the fix at different start
  timings ran four times as long without one.
- **Every build:** host tests pass, and the binaries are byte-for-byte what
  a clean build of the source produces.
